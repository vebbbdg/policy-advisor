Image embedding models convert pixels into vectors that capture visual similarity.
Downstream tasks such as deduplication, clustering, and visual search consume these vectors directly.
Convolutional neural networks and vision transformers can both be used as image encoders.
Vision transformers tend to scale better with data, while convolutions remain efficient on small inputs.

CLIP produces embeddings where images and their text captions are close together in the same vector space.
This alignment enables zero-shot classification by comparing an image against candidate label texts.
Image embeddings power visual search engines that find products that look alike.
Near-duplicate detection for moderation pipelines is another common production use case.

Text and image embeddings must be trained jointly when they need to share one vector space.
Contrastive training pulls matching image-text pairs together and pushes mismatched pairs apart.
Text embedding models for retrieval are evaluated differently, typically against labeled question-passage pairs.
Benchmark choice matters because an encoder that excels at classification may rank poorly for search.
