An embedding model converts text into a fixed-size vector of numbers that captures semantic meaning.
These vectors let machines compare pieces of text by geometry instead of by exact word overlap.
Sentences with similar meaning are mapped to vectors that are close together in the vector space.
Synonyms, paraphrases, and translations tend to land near each other when the model was trained on them.

The all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model produces embeddings with 384 dimensions and runs efficiently on CPU.
Its small size makes it a common default for prototypes, laptops, and CI pipelines.
Larger embedding models usually rank better but cost more memory and latency per query.
Embedding quality directly determines retrieval quality, so choosing the right model matters.
Benchmark suites such as MTEB publish comparative rankings that help narrow the candidate list.

Retrieval systems should use the same embedding model for documents and for queries.
Mixing models puts vectors in incompatible spaces and silently destroys retrieval quality.
If the model is ever upgraded, the whole collection must be re-embedded from scratch.
Multilingual embedding models are required when the corpus contains several languages.
Domain-specific corpora sometimes benefit from continued training of a general-purpose embedding model.
