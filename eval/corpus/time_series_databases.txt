Time-series databases are optimized for storing timestamped measurements such as sensor readings and metrics.
Workloads are dominated by append-only writes and range queries over recent windows of data.
InfluxDB is a popular time-series database used for monitoring and observability workloads.
Alternatives such as TimescaleDB extend relational engines with time-oriented storage and indexing.

Unlike general-purpose databases, time-series databases compress old data aggressively to save storage.
Values that change slowly can be stored as deltas, and repeated values are deduplicated entirely.
Downsampling reduces the resolution of old data so dashboards stay fast while keeping recent data at full precision.
Retention policies automatically expire data that is older than a configured horizon.

Time-series queries usually filter by time range first and then aggregate values inside the window.
Common aggregations include averages, percentiles, and rates of change between samples.
Alerting systems evaluate these queries continuously and page operators when thresholds are crossed.
Vector databases solve a different problem, ranking by similarity instead of ordering by timestamp.
