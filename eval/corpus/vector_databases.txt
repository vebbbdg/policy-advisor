Vector databases are specialized storage systems designed to hold and search high-dimensional embeddings.
They sit at the heart of modern semantic search, recommendation, and retrieval-augmented generation systems.
Unlike relational databases that match exact values, vector databases measure similarity between vectors.
The most common similarity metrics are cosine similarity and Euclidean distance.
Cosine similarity compares the angle between two vectors and ignores their length, which suits normalized text embeddings.
Euclidean distance measures straight-line separation and is sensitive to vector magnitude.
Choosing the wrong metric can silently degrade ranking quality, so it should be validated with an evaluation set.

Indexing is what makes similarity search fast at scale.
Approximate nearest neighbor algorithms such as HNSW trade a small amount of recall for much faster search.
HNSW builds a hierarchy of navigable small-world graphs so queries can skip large regions of the index.
Exact brute-force scan remains the gold standard for small collections because it never misses the true nearest neighbor.
Quantization compresses vectors into fewer bytes and reduces memory usage at the cost of a small accuracy drop.

Chroma is an open-source vector database that is popular for prototyping RAG applications.
It stores documents together with their vectors and metadata, which keeps bookkeeping simple for small teams.
Production deployments often move to managed services once data volume or throughput outgrows a single machine.

Metadata filtering lets you restrict similarity search to a subset of the collection.
For example, a support assistant might search only tickets tagged with the customer's product line.
Combining filters with vector search narrows the candidate set before ranking and often improves precision.

Vector databases scale by sharding the index across multiple machines when the collection grows large.
Replicas absorb read traffic and keep latency stable during query spikes.
Monitoring recall against a labeled query set is the most reliable way to detect index degradation over time.
