Fine-tuning adapts a pretrained language model to a narrow task by training it on labeled examples.
It changes the model weights themselves, so the new behavior ships with every future request.
LoRA reduces fine-tuning cost by training small adapter matrices instead of all model weights.
Adapters can be swapped at serving time, letting one base model host many specialized variants.

Retrieval-augmented generation is often cheaper than fine-tuning when the goal is adding new factual knowledge.
Documents can be updated instantly in a vector store, while fine-tuning requires a full training run.
Fine-tuning can still cause catastrophic forgetting of abilities the base model used to have.
Teams often combine both, fine-tuning for tone and format while retrieving for facts.

Evaluation data must be held out from training data to get an honest estimate of quality.
Leaking evaluation examples into training produces inflated scores that collapse in production.
Regression suites should rerun after every training job so degradations are caught early.
Embedding models can also be fine-tuned on domain pairs to sharpen retrieval relevance.
